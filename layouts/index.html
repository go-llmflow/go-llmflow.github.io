LLM serving for a shared GPU cluster,
in pure Go.

go-llmflow is high-performance LLM inference and serving in Go. Its first project, gateway, is an OpenAI-compatible gateway in front of vLLM for clusters that several institutions own together: each team gets its share, and the share a team leaves idle is lent to the others. The gateway

One OpenAI endpoint, many owners.

The gateway sits between your users' OpenAI clients and the vLLM instances of a cluster. v0.1.0 is released: llmflow-gateway has serve, export, check-config and version, and is configured in HCL2 with diagnostics down to file, line and column. Its open limitations (one replica per pool, no TLS) and its roadmap are in the documentation.

🔑

Virtual keys and teams

A key per user, attached to the team of its institution, so that every request is charged to the right share. Keys are stored hashed, teams, budgets and usage live in PostgreSQL (or in memory), and an admin API manages them.

💳

Budgets and rate limits

Budgets in GPU-hours per day, week or month, and per-key limits on requests, tokens and requests in flight. Budgets fail closed when the store cannot be read.

🔌

OpenAI-compatible

The request is rewritten on the way in, the response, a server-sent-events stream included, is passed through event by event, and token usage is captured for accounting. The parsers are fuzzed.

⚙️

GPU-hour accounting

Usage is counted per team in GPU-hours, the unit of Slurm's sreport, and exported as CSV to reconcile inference with batch. The gateway adds about 55 µs at p50 over a direct call (single core; method and caveats).

Fair-share

A guaranteed share, and the idle rest.

Static quotas leave the share of an absent team unused. The gateway reserves each team its share of a pool, and lets the others borrow what is idle. It does so with the one knob vLLM has: the priority of a request.

🎯

Within the share

While a team has fewer requests running than its reservation (its share times the pool's capacity), the request gets vLLM priority 0 (interactive) or 1 (batch).

🤝

Borrowing

Above the reservation the request borrows, at priority 10 (interactive) or 11 (batch) and above, the teams that consumed least first (the ranking is Slurm Fair Tree's level fairshare). vLLM admits within-share requests first and preempts borrowers first when KV-cache memory runs out.

🧪

Simulated

A deterministic simulator of vLLM's v1 priority scheduler checks the policy: with both teams saturating, the running fraction follows the shares (0.79 and 0.21 against 0.8125 and 0.1875), and a control with every request at priority 0 does not.

Security

The client never sets its own priority.

A priority a client can choose is a priority every client chooses. The gateway removes priority, extra_body, request_id, kv_transfer_params, ec_transfer_params and vllm_xargs from the request before it reaches vLLM, and decides the priority itself.

🧹

Re-encoded, not patched

The body is re-encoded from its parsed form, so duplicate keys and escaped spellings of a field name cannot reach vLLM. A fuzz target checks that no stripped field survives.

📄

Strict configuration

Unknown keys are rejected, upstream URLs are limited to http and https without credentials, and secrets are read from files only, never from the command line.
